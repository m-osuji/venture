How to Play Venture

Venture is a strategy game about controlling markets, building influence, and outplaying your rivals. It takes inspiration from Risk and Diplomacy, but replaces luck with business knowledge. When two teams clash over a market, the winner is decided by quiz performance and smart preparation rather than dice rolls.

Your job is to grow your influence, protect valuable markets, and choose the right moments to negotiate, invest, and strike. Every round matters because your short-term choices shape your long-term position on the board.

What To Know First

Before you start, keep these core ideas in mind:

  • Markets generate Influence Points, or IP, at the end of each round.
  • IP is allocated to markets during the plan stage.
  • There is no separate defend action. A market is defended by the IP already placed on it.
  • Attacks are launched from markets where you have already invested IP.
  • Negotiation matters because what you say you will do and what you actually do can affect your ethical score.

Core Rule

There is no separate defend action in Venture. If you want to protect a market, you must place IP there during the plan stage.

Round Structure

Each game begins with a quick opening quiz to decide team order. After that, every round follows the same five-stage cycle. All teams move through the stages together, so timing, planning, and negotiation all matter.

Plan

Allocate IP across your markets. That IP both protects the market it sits on and gives you the power to attack from it later.

  • Choose where to build strength.
  • Note your intended strategy for the round.
  • Set up both attack options and defense.
Negotiate

Talk to other teams, build alliances, make promises, or bluff your way into a better position.

  • Declare what you say you will do.
  • Offer alliances, support, or deals.
  • Remember that betrayal can hurt your ethics.
Orders

Lock in your real moves. These are the actions that actually happen once negotiation ends.

  • Attack from a market with allocated IP.
  • Research one of your own markets.
  • Hold if you want to conserve position.
Resolve

Contested markets are settled by quiz performance plus the board state you built earlier in the round.

  • Answer market-themed questions.
  • Use preparation to boost your effective strength.
  • Win the market if your side comes out ahead.
Update

The game state refreshes. Ownership changes, research applies, and fresh IP is distributed.

  • Refresh the leaderboard.
  • Apply upgrades and synergies.
  • Record ethical consequences from the round.

Actions And IP

Influence Points are the main resource in Venture. You earn them from the markets you control, spend them to strengthen your position, and commit them when you attack or invest in research.

In practical terms, IP is used in three main ways:

  • Allocation: place IP into your markets during planning.
  • Attack commitment: use allocated IP from a source market to contest another market.
  • Research: spend IP to improve a market you already control.

The key idea is that IP on the board matters just as much as IP in reserve. Over-invest in one area and you may become vulnerable elsewhere. Spread too thin and you may lack the strength to win important contests.

Market Attributes

Every market has its own mix of value, risk, and strategic importance. These attributes shape how rewarding a market is and how difficult it is to hold.

Size

Size determines the base amount of IP a market generates. Larger markets are more valuable, but they are also more likely to become targets.

Growth Potential

Growth potential reflects how much a market can improve over time. High growth markets may become much stronger if you invest in them, making them powerful long-term assets.

Regulation Level

Regulation represents legal complexity and compliance pressure. Highly regulated markets are harder to improve, but they are also harder to take, which makes them useful defensive anchors.

Security Risk

Security risk represents instability and vulnerability. Riskier markets can become hotspots for conflict because they offer attractive rewards, but they are harder to defend consistently.

Research And Development

Research lets you improve a market instead of launching an attack. Depending on the option chosen, research can increase production, reduce regulatory burden, improve security, or permanently fortify a market.

Market Synergies

Some markets work especially well together. Owning connected markets can unlock synergy bonuses such as extra IP, stronger positions, or other strategic advantages. This rewards players who pursue a coherent market plan rather than capturing territories at random.

How To Win

Venture rewards more than brute expansion. To win, you need to balance growth, defense, timing, and diplomacy. Strong players know when to bank IP, when to invest it, when to negotiate honestly, and when to take a calculated risk.

The final result is based on overall performance across the game, including IP, market control, and ethical score. That means reckless betrayal may help in the short term, but it can still cost you in the final standings.

A strong Venture strategy usually does three things well:

  • builds a stable IP economy
  • contests the right markets at the right time
  • uses negotiation without losing control of the bigger picture

Winning Tips

  • Do not spread your IP too thin unless the board is quiet.
  • Use negotiation to shape the round, but expect promises to be tested.
  • Target markets that improve your long-term synergy, not just your short-term score.
